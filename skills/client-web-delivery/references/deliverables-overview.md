# Deliverables around the pitch: the overview

Moved here word for word from `SKILL.md` (v1.25.0).

When the pitch site is live and passes the standard above, build what the seller takes to the owner: a two-page **plan poster** (page 1: the price, what's included, a real photo of the client's work with screenshots of the live pitch, a "what changes" comparison checked on their current site, and a logo QR to the live pitch; page 2: why it's worth the monthly price, with a problem the owner hasn't noticed yet, one sourced stat, six to eight service cards and two more sections of the live site), plus a **contract-questions sheet** with one- or two-sentence answers on money, edits, canceling, ownership, what's not included and getting started. Method, render commands and checks: `references/pitch-kit.md`. Templates: `assets/pitch-poster-template.html`, `assets/pitch-poster-page2-template.html`, `assets/contract-questions-template.md`. Scripts: `scripts/shots.py` (screenshots), `scripts/make_qr.py` (fails unless the QR decodes at every size), `scripts/render_pages.py` (PNGs, and one PDF built from them so it looks the same on a phone, with checks). Every poster line is written from `copy-bank` research (owners' own words, opened and linked in `deliverables/pitch/poster-language.md`), never from the examples. The pipeline setup installs what they need.

- **The proposal** (`references/proposal.md`, `assets/proposal-template.html`): one page with what it costs and how to say yes, every number from the builder profile's `offer`, blanks where the builder hasn't set a term, and no tool or vendor of the builder's named.
- **The launch reel** (`references/launch-reel.md`, `scripts/reel.py`, `assets/reel-stat-card.html`): the real pitch filmed scrolling, played inside a phone drawn in code, joined with cards animated in code. No generated video, no credits, no sound.
- **Local numbers** (`scripts/census.py`): county figures for stat cards from the Census, with the Census's own label and the source line, once the builder's free key is in the profile.
- **Then the hype set** (`references/hype-set.md`): the images that make an owner want the site before the meeting. A coming-soon card, three to five stat cards (one number each, local first, the source on the card), phone cards showing the real pitch, a then-and-now card, and an opt-in coming-soon page. **Instagram mockups come first** (`references/mockups.md`): the logo as the profile picture, the launch post and the site in the bio, drawn in code from templates. Templates: `assets/ig-post-image.html`, `assets/ig-post-mockup.html`, `assets/ig-profile-mockup.html`, `assets/hype-photo-card.html` (words over a real photo), `assets/hype-soon-card.html`, `assets/hype-stat-card.html`, `assets/hype-phone-card.html`. `scripts/render_cards.py` renders each at post and story size and fails on overflow, a missing image or a leftover placeholder. Hooks can be loud; numbers are exact and sourced. Krea makes only the setting, one generation each, quoted first.
- Everything sent to the client by hand lives in the project's **`deliverables/` folder, which is gitignored** (owner sheet, pitch kit, mockups, hype set, handoff). It is where the builder checks anything before it goes to a person, and nothing in it reaches an owner as Markdown: what goes out is a PDF or an image. If it is already tracked, ignore it and `git rm -r --cached deliverables`. Tell the builder where it is; git does not back it up.
- Prices, contract terms and what's included are the builder's. Draft defaults only where they haven't said, and list each one as a suggestion to confirm. Put no results on the poster (rankings, sales), and no capability a vendor hasn't confirmed.
- Nothing goes to the client until the builder says it's ready.
