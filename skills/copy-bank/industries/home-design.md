# Residential building design and custom homes (designers, drafters, design/build)

The buyer is a family planning a custom home (often the first one, often on a lot they already own), or a home builder looking for a design partner to send clients to. The designer draws the plans, the permit set and usually 3D renderings; a builder builds. In Texas many firms are "building designers", not licensed architects, and must never be called architects.

First filled 2026-11-05 for a Rio Grande Valley (Brownsville, TX) building-design pitch.

## What the themes say

- The strongest theme is **being heard and guided**, not the look of the house. Reviews praise patience, comfort, answers to questions, being walked through a process they didn't understand.
- Second is **seeing the dream become a design**: buyers arrive with ideas and no style vocabulary, and value leaving with a design that's theirs.
- **Not like the neighbors:** buyers who choose custom design are reacting against tract and stock homes.
- **Cost fear** keeps people from asking at all. A free first consultation removes it; price claims shouldn't be made.
- "Dream home" is everywhere in buyer words and in every competitor's headline. Use the buyers' specific form ("the dream in our heads", "crazy ideas") or the outcome, never "your dream home".

## Hidden problem, and the honest payoff

| Hidden problem | Honest payoff (what a building designer actually does) |
|---|---|
| A catalog or builder's standard plan won't fit their lot or how they live | A plan drawn for their lot and family |
| They can't picture a house from flat drawings; changes during the build cost more | They see it, and walk it, in 3D before anything is built |
| They don't know where to start, or what it costs to ask | A free first meeting where someone listens |
| Setbacks, variances and city review stall projects | A permit-ready set and someone who handles the paperwork |
| Builders can't find a designer who treats their clients well | A design partner their clients like working with |
